The Situation

Buyers are calling HVAC owners.

Over the last several years, private-equity-backed platforms have been buying residential and commercial HVAC companies across the country. Some of the largest are based in Dallas-Fort Worth, and they have bought companies here. For a lot of Texas owners, the first conversation about selling starts with an unsolicited call, not a plan.

An offer is rarely just a check. It can include cash at closing, rollover equity in the buyer’s platform, an earnout tied to future results, and an employment agreement that keeps you running the company for a few years. Each piece changes what you actually take home, when you get it, and how it is taxed.

Your company is probably most of your net worth. That makes the sale the biggest financial decision of your life, and the time to plan for it is before the offer arrives.

What We Do

Before a sale

Put a number on the life you want after HVAC, then see how the business, your real estate and your other assets add up against it. If there is a gap, you find out while there is still time to close it.

When an offer comes in

Look at an offer in terms of what you keep. Cash at closing, rollover equity and an earnout are not worth the same, and they are not taxed the same. We help you see the difference and bring the right questions to your CPA and attorney.

The Decision Lab

Five key decisions.

Our process is built around five decisions. For an HVAC owner, they turn a sale from a transaction into a plan for the rest of your life.

1

Decide who you are.

Before you look at an offer, get clear on what matters to you. Some owners want to step away completely. Others want to stay on and run the company for a new owner for a few years. Your values shape which deal is right for you.

2

Decide where you are.

Know where you stand today: the business, the building, retirement accounts, debt, and what your life actually costs. You cannot judge an offer until you know what it has to do for you.

3

Decide where you are going.

What does life after HVAC look like? We help you define it and put a number on it: how much you need to walk away with, after taxes, to fund it.

4

Decide how you are going to get there.

Close the gap between what the business could bring and what you need. That can mean working on the things buyers pay for, planning with your CPA and attorney ahead of the deal, and building an investment plan for the proceeds.

5

Decide who matters.

Family, key employees, your community, the causes you care about. Decide who your wealth should serve, and plan for it early, while more options are open.

Market Momentum

Buyers are active in Texas HVAC.

Private-equity-backed platforms keep buying HVAC and home service companies, and Texas is on their map. That says nothing about what any one company will sell for. It does mean that owners who are prepared, with clean books, recurring revenue and a team that runs without them, tend to have more options when a buyer calls.
