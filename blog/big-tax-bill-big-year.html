A Big Tax Bill Means You Had a Big Year

By RJ Finley · December 2, 2026

A lot of people will soon get a 2026 tax bill projection from their CPA and feel their stomach drop. I get it, because nobody likes writing a big check to the government. But a big tax bill only happens when success is involved, and that’s easy to forget when you’re staring at the number.

The $100,000 purchase nobody needed

Picture an owner who sees a big projected bill in early December and buys $100,000 of equipment because of the bonus depreciation rules. These numbers are made up, so say his tax rate on the next dollar is 30% (yours will differ). The purchase saves him $30,000 in taxes, but it costs $100,000, so the equipment really costs him $70,000 he’d otherwise have kept.

The real question is whether he’d have spent $70,000 on it if there were no tax benefit. Sometimes the answer is yes. However, a lot of times it is no.

Why the bill feels bigger than the profit

The bill shows up as one number, but the profit behind it was spread across a year of payroll, rent, and long days. Psychologists have a phrase for this. It is called loss aversion. People feel a loss more sharply than a gain of the same size, and a tax payment feels like a loss even though it came from a year you spent growing.

Start with the dog

You’ve heard that the tail shouldn’t wag the dog. Here, the dog is the life you’re building for your family, and the tail is the tax bill. It can change how things feel, but it shouldn’t decide where you go.

That doesn’t mean ignoring taxes. Retirement contributions, charitable giving, and a talk with your CPA before December 31 can all be worth it, as long as they’re things you already wanted to do.

What matters is the order. If you start with "how do I pay less," the tax code ends up choosing what you do with your money. If you start with what you want the money to do, your talk with the CPA gets much more useful.
