Wealth & Significance

Transforming wealth to significance — what it actually means.

By Sanger D. Smith, CEPA™ September 2026 7 min read

People seek significance. I think we all do — not always consciously, but it's there. And one of the things people really fear when they retire or leave a business is losing it.

That fear is why I wrote my book "A Life Rich with Significance." And it's why my dad Shawn and I sat down for one of my favorite episodes of The Decidedly Podcast to talk about what this phrase actually means in practice.

Success doesn't mean fulfillment.

In my career, I meet people at varying levels of success. Some are extraordinarily financially successful. Some are struggling. And here's what I've found: the people who are financially successful are not always fulfilled.

That surprised me early on. You'd think more money equals more happiness. But it doesn't work that way. What I found is that fulfillment requires a key element: significance. A sense that your life and your wealth are actually doing something meaningful.

Without significance, wealth is just a number on a statement. With it, wealth becomes a tool for impact.

What significance looks like.

Significance isn't one thing. It's different for every family I work with. For some, it's funding their grandchildren's education. For others, it's supporting a cause they believe in. For some, it's coaching the next generation of entrepreneurs in their community.

But there's a pattern I've noticed across hundreds of conversations with business owners:

  • Significance is relational — it almost always involves other people
  • Significance is intentional — it doesn't happen by accident
  • Significance requires clarity — you have to know what matters to you before you can pursue it

That last point is why we built the Decision Lab. The first of our five key decisions is "Decide Who You Are." Not who your business is. Who you are. Because if you don't know that, no amount of money will make you feel significant.

My dad's story.

My dad Shawn spent decades in financial services. Built a successful practice. Served families. Had everything you'd look at from the outside and say "that guy made it."

But the transition out of that daily work — the part where you're no longer the person people call, no longer the person making the decisions — that was the hard part. Not the money. The identity.

What saved him was having already thought about what significance looked like after the business. For us, it turned out to be the podcast. Over 750 episodes of sitting together, talking about decisions, interviewing incredible people. It's been one of the best things in both our lives.

But that didn't happen by accident. It happened because we were intentional about creating something meaningful together.

The wealth-to-significance framework.

Here's how I help clients make this shift:

  1. Decide who you are. Strip away the business title. What's left? What drives you? What kind of person do you want to be remembered as?
  2. Decide where you are. Get honest about your finances, your relationships, and your energy. What's working? What's depleting you?
  3. Decide where you're going. Not just financially — what does a meaningful Tuesday look like five years from now? What does your family look like? Your community involvement?
  4. Decide how you'll get there. Now we can talk strategy. Investment allocation, estate planning, business transition — all in service of the life you actually want.
  5. Decide who matters. Your family, your employees, your community, your causes. How does your wealth serve them — not just after you're gone, but right now?

A lot of people seek significance. They just never put a plan around it. And without a plan, it stays a wish instead of a reality.

Money is an amoral tool.

I say this to clients all the time: money is an amoral tool. It's inherently neither good nor bad. It's simply a resource. What makes it meaningful is what you decide to do with it.

You can use wealth to build legacy, to serve others, to create experiences with the people you love. Or you can just accumulate it and hope it means something. The difference is a decision.

That's what "transforming wealth to significance" means. It's not a slogan. It's a framework. And it starts with a conversation.
