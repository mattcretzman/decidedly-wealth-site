I Thought I'd Feel Free

By RJ Finley · October 7, 2026

Mark sold his logistics company after 25 years of building it.

He'd worked toward that moment for over a decade. The number on the wire was exactly what he'd hoped for, maybe more. And then, about three months later, he called me and said something I'll never forget:

“RJ, I thought I'd feel free. I don't feel free. I don't know what I feel.”

What Mark was experiencing has a name, though most people don't know it until they're already in the middle of it. It's the emotional aftermath of an exit, and it's more common than anyone in the financial world wants to talk about.

The Captain Steps Off the Ship

When you've built a company, your identity doesn't just live in the business. It IS the business. The way people see you, the way you see yourself, the rhythm of your days, the problems that fill your mind at 6 a.m. All of it is wrapped up in what you've created.

Then you sell it. And suddenly, the ship keeps sailing. It just sails without you.

What Mark lost wasn't just a company. He lost the sense of purpose that got him out of bed in the morning. He lost his community, the team he'd coached and protected for years. He lost the feeling of being needed, of being the one who makes the call. And without any of that, he didn't quite know who he was anymore.

And on top of all that, he'd just become the wealthiest he'd ever been. Which added its own quiet pressure: “I have everything I ever wanted. So why doesn't this feel like I thought it would?”

The problem isn't the money. The problem is that no one plans the chapter after the chapter. We spend years planning the exit. The deal structure, the tax strategy, the valuation, the timing. We spend almost no time planning what life actually looks like...
