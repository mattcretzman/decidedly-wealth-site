Business Valuation

Know what your Fort Worth business is worth.

Most business owners have never had a professional valuation. That means they’re making every major decision — from growth strategy to exit timing — without knowing the most important number.

Why Valuation Matters

You wouldn’t sell your house without an appraisal.

Yet most business owners have never had their company professionally valued. They’re guessing — and guessing almost always means leaving money on the table or making decisions based on a number that isn’t real.

A business valuation isn’t just for selling. It’s essential for succession planning, estate planning, buy-sell agreements, partner buyouts, divorce proceedings, and simply understanding where you stand financially.

At Decidedly Wealth Management, we don’t just tell you a number. We help you understand why your business is worth what it is — and what you can do to increase that value before you need it most.

When You Need a Valuation

Six scenarios that require knowing your number.

Before Selling

Exit Planning

If you’re thinking about selling in the next 1-7 years, you need to know what you’re working with. A valuation today gives you time to increase value before going to market.

Family Transition

Succession Planning

Transferring a business to the next generation requires a fair valuation for tax purposes, ownership structuring, and ensuring equitable treatment of all family members.

Partner Changes

Buy-Sell Agreements

When a partner leaves, retires, or passes away, a current valuation protects everyone. Outdated buy-sell agreements are one of the most common sources of business disputes.

How It Works

Three approaches to business valuation.

Income Approach

Values the business based on its ability to generate future income. This includes discounted cash flow (DCF) and capitalization of earnings methods. Most common for profitable, established businesses.

Market Approach

Compares your business to similar businesses that have recently sold. Like real estate comps, this gives you a benchmark based on what buyers are actually paying in your industry.

Asset Approach

Values the business based on the fair market value of its assets minus liabilities. Most relevant for asset-heavy businesses or companies being liquidated.

Common Questions

Business valuation FAQ.

How much does a business valuation cost?

It depends on the complexity. A formal appraisal from a certified business appraiser can cost $5,000-$25,000+. An advisory-level valuation through our Decision Lab process gives you a clear picture of your value and what drives it — often at a fraction of the cost.

How long does a valuation take?

A formal appraisal typically takes 4-8 weeks. An advisory valuation discussion through our process can happen in your first few meetings. Either way, it starts with a conversation.

Do I need a valuation before selling my business?

Absolutely. Going to market without knowing your value is like selling a house without an appraisal. You might accept too little — or price yourself out of the market entirely.

What’s the difference between a formal appraisal and advisory valuation?

A formal appraisal is a legal document prepared by a certified appraiser (ASA, ABV, CVA) — required for tax filings, court proceedings, and regulatory purposes. An advisory valuation is a strategic assessment that helps you understand your value and plan accordingly. Most business owners benefit from the advisory approach first.
